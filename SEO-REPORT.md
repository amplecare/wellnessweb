## 2–7. Keyword, intent, audience, CTA and conversion path mapping

All of this lives in [`content/seo/keywordMap.ts`](content/seo/keywordMap.ts) as typed
data rather than in this document. Each entry carries `primaryKeyword`,
`secondaryKeywords`, `intent`, `audience`, `conversionGoal`, `primaryCta`,
`internalLinks`, `title`, `description` and `distinctFrom`.

It is a module rather than a spreadsheet for a specific reason: page metadata, the
sitemap and the internal-link blocks are all generated from it, so the documented
architecture and the shipped site cannot drift apart.

**Coverage:** 15 pages, 69 keywords, zero duplicates.

**Conversion path, every page:** search intent → recognition → emotional turn →
consequence → education → Ample Care solution → sourced evidence → objection handling →
FAQs → cluster links → final CTA. Seven `/book-consultation` links per landing page
(brief asked for five).

## 8. Internal links

## 9. SEO metadata

## 13. SEO opportunities discovered

1. **The positioning gap is real and unoccupied.** Generic consultancies understand
   wellbeing but not care; care specialists understand care but sell HR and staffing.
   Nobody credibly owns "wellbeing measurement for social care". The site is built
   around that gap.
2. **Competitors lead with scale** ("2,000 organisations", "200 UK businesses"). Ample
   Care cannot win that and should not try. Specificity beats scale here — a page that
   describes a night handover accurately outperforms a client-count claim.
3. **Sector-specific long tail is largely uncontested.** "domiciliary care staff
   wellbeing" and "supported living staff wellbeing" have no dedicated specialist page
   among the competitors reviewed.
4. **Eight service keywords previously had no destination.** Now fixed.
5. **Skills for Care ranks for retention advice but sells nothing** — a citation and
   complement opportunity rather than a competitive threat.

## 14. Conversion opportunities discovered

1. Every CTA previously landed on `/contact`, a general page. Now a purpose-built
   `/book-consultation` that removes risk rather than selling.
2. Sticky mobile CTA — the audience reads on a phone between shifts and rarely reaches
   the page bottom.
3. Objection handling was absent site-wide. Six objections now handled on the home page
   and per page, never by disparaging the reader's existing HR team or EAP.
4. Articles previously would have been dead ends; each carries a topic-specific CTA.
5. Pricing CTAs now carry the tier through: `/book-consultation?tier=…`.

### Articles deliberately not written

The brief listed twelve articles. Ten were written. Two were dropped because writing
them would have breached the brief's own cannibalisation rule (§21):

- **"What Is Workforce Wellbeing?"** — the pillar page `/care-workforce-wellbeing`
  exists to answer exactly this. A second page on the same intent would split the
  authority of the strongest page on the site.
- **"How to Improve Care Staff Wellbeing"** — same searcher intent as the pillar, which
  already answers it at length.

If either is wanted later, strengthen the pillar rather than adding a competing page.
The reasoning is recorded in `content/resourcesStrategy.ts` so it is not undone by
accident.

## 19. Keyword cannibalisation risks

Resolved by assigning distinct angles, recorded in `distinctFrom` on each entry:

- **retention** — the people who stay, and why they might not
- **turnover** — the cost and pattern of those who already left
- **morale** — how it feels on shift right now
- **burnout** — the mechanism underneath all three

"care staff wellbeing" and "social care workforce wellbeing" are *not* distinct intents,
so they share the one pillar page rather than competing.

**Watch:** `/care-worker-burnout` vs `/care-manager-wellbeing` (frontline vs management —
currently distinct); `/workforce-wellbeing-assessment` vs
`/services/staff-wellbeing-assessments` (the broad keyword vs the specific deliverable —
keep the service page narrower); sector pages vs the pillar (each must keep its
setting-specific content or it becomes a thin duplicate).

## Editorial rules applied throughout

Worth preserving, because they are what keep this defensible for a healthcare service:

- **No invented statistics.** Figures come from
  [`content/seo/evidence.ts`](content/seo/evidence.ts) by id; `getEvidence()` throws on
  an unknown id, so an unsourced figure fails the build rather than shipping.
- **No fabricated testimonials, case studies or client logos.**
- **No causal claims the evidence cannot support.** Wellbeing pressure and turnover move
  together; saying one *causes* the other is a claim we cannot make. Copy uses "linked
  to", "commonly", "can".
- **No guarantees of outcome.** Several pages say explicitly that we cannot promise a
  turnover reduction, and that anyone who does should be treated with suspicion.
- **Objections never disparage the reader's existing HR team, EAP or provider.** They
  chose those; attacking them insults the reader's judgement.
